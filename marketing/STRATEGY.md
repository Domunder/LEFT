# Marketing strategy

Rewritten 2026-11-21 with the user, replacing the plan of 2026-11-18 (eight
accounts, 45 posts a week, published by API): **three accounts, posted by
hand by the user, everything before the posting done by Claude.** Still free
to start, still every account disclosed "by LEFT", still stock graded with
LEFT's looks until real events exist.

**Two channels:**
1. **Founder outreach to hosts** (`outreach/README.md`) — unchanged. The
   fastest way to real events, and real events are the content that works.
2. **The three content accounts below** — a test bench for hooks and formats.
   Judged by store visits and events created, not views.

## Goal

Downloads and created events — not followers. A post works if people search
for LEFT, visit leftmoment.com or create a moment.

## What works in this category (researched 2026-11-21)

Sources are third-party write-ups, so treat numbers as estimates.

- **The app is never the subject.** POV's biggest posts are wedding stories
  and opinions ("five controversial rules we had at our wedding") with the
  app as one item or the last slide. A plain app demo wins for nobody.
- **Photo slideshows carry the category.** POV's wedding slideshows are
  reported at 0.8–1.5M views each; cheap to make, easy to vary.
- **Niche accounts, many hooks.** POV runs over a dozen niche accounts. We
  cannot post that much by hand, so we copy the method (one niche per
  account, many hook variants), not the volume.
- **People who attend many weddings are a channel.** ONCE courts wedding
  planners and wedding content creators. That is outreach, not content —
  a later extension of channel 1.
- **Sound off.** Most Instagram placements play muted: on-screen text carries
  the post.
- **Real footage beats staged.** In the wedding niche real client footage
  outperforms polished ads. Until we have it, we say "idea" and "POV".

## Feelings first (the user, 2026-11-21)

**We sell the morning after, not a camera app.** Every post is a small story
with a feeling at its centre; LEFT is how the story happens, named late and
once. A post that could run unchanged as a feature list is rewritten.

The feelings that belong to this product:

| Feeling | The story |
|---|---|
| Anticipation | the night is over and nobody has seen a single photo yet |
| Surprise | the photo you did not know anyone took — grandma dancing, your dad crying |
| Being seen | your wedding through 80 pairs of eyes instead of one photographer's |
| Presence | ten shots make you choose; the phone goes back in the pocket |
| Belonging | everyone opens the same album at the same minute |
| Play | a secret mission sends shy guests to people they have never met |

**How LEFT appears in a post (the user, 2026-11-21, after research):**
- **The app shows up once, mid-story, as a real screen** (`screen:` with
  `text:`) at the beat it explains — the developing screen where the story
  says nobody can look, the album where the photos develop, the mission
  list where a guest gets a card.
- **The last slide is the payoff**, a photo and the feeling — never a brand
  or ad card. Slideshows are judged on how many swipe to the end and save;
  an ad at the end is where people drop off.
- **No logo or watermark on the pictures.** The handle under every post
  already says who it is from, and a corner logo makes each slide read as an
  ad (Instagram also says a watermark is no "creative input").
- **The caption names LEFT once**, with the link in bio.
- The control post (`weddings-4`) stays a plain "what LEFT is" post on
  purpose, so the pilot shows whether soft selling actually wins.

**Instagram's originality rule (April 2026):** accounts that mostly post
other people's photos without meaningful creative input are no longer
recommended to non-followers. Our grade, story and text should count, but an
account built only on stock is exposed — one more reason real event photos
replace stock as early as possible.

**How a story stays honest on stock:** it is told in the second person or as
an idea ("you wake up the morning after. 312 photos you have never seen."),
never as something that happened to a named or pictured person. Story
templates the writer uses:

1. **The morning after** — the night ends → nothing to see → the film
   develops → the photos nobody expected.
2. **The photo you didn't know existed** — one moment the couple missed,
   caught by a guest.
3. **What the photographer missed** — the official pictures vs. the guests'.
4. **The mission** — one guest, one card, one unlikely photo.
5. **Ten shots** — what you would spend them on.

## The pilot — prove the format before the full cadence

Nothing below (three accounts, 12 assets a week) starts until the pilot
passes. It is built to cost the user about five minutes a day.

- **Two accounts, two strategies** (the user, 2026-11-21), each on TikTok
  and Instagram, one post a day each for 7 days. The posts exist:
  `posts/pilot/` (its README is the posting list), rendered into
  `queue/pilot/`.
  - **`left.weddings` — story slideshows.** Templates 1, 2 and 4, each with
    two different hooks, plus one plain feature post as the control ("what
    LEFT is", app screens). If stories do not beat the control, "feelings
    first" is wrong and we learn it in a week.
  - **`left.afterdark` — one picture, one line** (format 4): the cheapest
    post there is. Tests whether a single relatable line does as well as a
    six-slide story.
- **Nothing to film or record** — the finished pictures and captions are
  handed over.
- **Reading the two against each other:** the accounts differ in audience
  *and* format, so views are not comparable. Compare the rates: saves +
  shares per view, profile visits per view. `left.afterdark` has no control;
  it passes on the first two bars alone.
- **Numbers, read 48 hours after each post** (the user pastes screenshots of
  the insights): views, saves, shares, profile visits, and on TikTok how many
  swiped to the last slide if shown.
- **Decision on day 9.** These bars are ours — starting points, not industry
  benchmarks — because a new account's view counts say little on their own:

| Result | Bar | Then |
|---|---|---|
| **Pass** | at least 2 posts with saves + shares ≥ 1 % of views, at least 1 post clearly above the rest in views (≥ 3× the median), and stories beat the control | full cadence; the winning template becomes half of all posts |
| **Mixed** | one of the three | a second 7-day round on the best template with new hooks |
| **Fail** | none | change the format (video, real footage), not the volume |

Views under ~300 on every post mean the account is not being distributed at
all — that is an account problem, not a content verdict; the round is
repeated before anything is concluded.

The user's reaction video (format 3) is tested right after the pilot, as one
post against the then-known median, because it is the only format that costs
the user recording time.

## The accounts

Three accounts, the same handle on Instagram and TikTok (six profiles).
Start with the first two; add the third when a week of posting takes less
than 15 minutes a day.

| # | Account | Audience | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `left.weddings` | couples 25–35, maids of honour, planners | **The main test bench.** Weddings bring the paying hosts. |
| 2 | `leftmoment` | everyone who heard of LEFT | The destination: what the app is, the reveal, missions, launch news. Low volume, never empty. |
| 3 | `left.afterdark` | 18–28, birthdays, house parties, trips with friends | Volume and young users; tests whether parties convert at all. |

`left.roadtrip` is parked (its file stays in `accounts/`): trip ideas run as
a test on `left.afterdark` and get their own account only if they win there.

## Formats

One asset is made once and posted on both platforms of its account.

| # | Format | What it is | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Story slideshow** | 5–7 slides. Slide 1 is an opinion, a rule or an idea; LEFT appears in the middle or at the end as part of the story. | all, about half of all posts |
| 2 | **List slideshow** | "7 photos every wedding forgets", "5 wedding rules we would set again" — one per slide, LEFT is one of them. | weddings, afterdark |
| 3 | **Silent reaction → reveal** (video, 8–12 s) | A face reacting (1.5 s) with a text hook → screen recording of the real app: a mission card, "3 left", the film developing, the album. No voice. | weddings, official |
| 4 | **POV single print** | One graded print, one line. | afterdark |
| 5 | **App walk-through** | Screens of the real app, one feature per slide. | official only |

**Rules for format 3** (the user's idea, 2026-11-21): the text is a POV or an
idea ("POV: you find out your guests get secret photo missions"), never a
first-person claim by someone who did not have that wedding — that is a fake
testimonial (`CLAUDE.md` rules 1–3). The face is the user, a friend who
agrees, or a licensed stock clip logged in `stock/LICENSES.md`. The second
half always shows the reveal or a mission, never aimless scrolling.

**Hook families** — every post belongs to one, so results compare families,
not single posts:

| Family | Example |
|---|---|
| Rule / opinion | "5 rules we'd set at our wedding again" |
| Mission (the game) | "we'd give every guest a secret photo mission" |
| Constraint | "10 photos each. for the whole wedding." |
| Delay | "nobody sees a single photo until the next morning" |
| Problem | "200 guests, 200 phones, 0 photos of the two of you" |
| Idea list | "wedding ideas your guests will actually talk about" |
| Nostalgia / POV | "POV: it's 2004 and you're waiting for your film" |

## How we test

- **One question per account per week** (e.g. "rule hooks or mission hooks on
  left.weddings?"). The plan names it; everything else stays the same.
- **A new idea is posted as three hook variants** on different days — same
  slides, different first slide. The hook decides most of the result.
- **Read a post after 48 hours**, a week after seven days.
- **Winner** = at least twice the account's median views *and* above-median
  saves + shares. A winner is remade three times the following week (new
  photos, same hook; same photos, new hook; as the other format).
- **Cut** a hook family after six posts under the account's median. Cut an
  account after six weeks if it is the weakest on profile visits and link
  clicks.
- **Paid only for proven posts:** a winner may be boosted (TikTok Spark Ad /
  Instagram boost) with a small budget. The user decides each time. No ad is
  made from scratch.

## Cadence

| Account | Assets a week | Posts (× 2 platforms) |
|---|---|---|
| `left.weddings` | 5 | 10 |
| `left.afterdark` | 5 | 10 |
| `leftmoment` | 2 | 4 |

12 assets, 24 posts, about 10 minutes a day. Posting times: 18:00–21:00 on
weekdays, 11:00–13:00 on weekends, for the main audience. Sounds are picked
in the app from the platform's own library when posting.

## Language

English on every account. Never mix languages on one account.

## Weekly loop

Sunday is the only day that needs thought; the rest is posting.

1. **Results (the user, 5 min):** paste the week's numbers per post into
   Claude — screenshots of the insights pages are enough. Claude writes
   `results/YYYY-Www.md`.
2. **`trend-researcher`** → `trends/YYYY-MM-DD.md`.
3. **`content-strategist`** → `plans/YYYY-Www.md`: 12 assets, the week's test
   question per account, what to cut, which winners to remake.
4. **`content-writer`** → `posts/YYYY-Www/*.md`: slide text, caption,
   hashtags, photos, look, a sound suggestion and the posting time.
5. **Renderer** (`cd web && npm run social -- YYYY-Www`) →
   `queue/YYYY-Www/{post id}/`: `tiktok/1.jpg … n.jpg` (1080×1920),
   `instagram/…` (1080×1350) and `caption.txt`, ready to send to the phone.
6. **The user posts** each day, or schedules the week at once where the
   platform's own scheduler takes the format. Posting is the approval:
   nothing goes out that the user did not look at.

For a format-3 video the post file is a shot list; the user records the
screen on their phone (the test event `DEVTEST`) and cuts it in the
platform's editor.

## Automation

| Step | State | How |
|---|---|---|
| Trend research, planning, writing | agents ready | one scheduled Claude task on Sunday runs all three |
| Rendering | built 2026-11-21 | `web/scripts/social-posts.mjs`: grades the photos with the app's look recipes (read from `looks.ts`), lays out the slides with an installed Chrome. Photos come from `stock/` or the website's `web/src/assets/photos/` |
| Results | by hand (paste) | reading insights by API comes with publishing |
| Publishing | by hand, **on purpose** | Meta Graph API and TikTok Content Posting API only once a format has clearly won — the setup (business accounts, developer apps, TikTok's audit) is not worth it for a test |

**Only the user can:** create the accounts, post, and decide on paid boosts.
Claude does not create accounts or handle passwords.

## Phases

0. **Pilot (9 days):** see "The pilot". One account, 7 slideshows, a decision
   on numbers.
1. **Test (6 weeks after a passed pilot):** the cadence above. Find one
   account and two hook families that work.
2. **Real content (as early as possible, in parallel):** one or two real
   events on LEFT with friends, with everyone's permission. Real reveals
   replace stock as the main content.
3. **Scale the winner:** more posts on the winning account, publishing by
   API, paid boosts, wedding creators and planners (as ONCE does).

Nothing may claim the app is available on a store where it is not yet.

## Costs

€0 until a post has won. Claude on the existing plan, free stock (Unsplash,
Pexels), free trend sources.

## Metrics

Per post (after 48 h): views, saves, shares, profile visits, link clicks.
Per week and account: follower growth, store visits (link in bio with
`?utm_source=tiktok&utm_campaign=left.weddings`). Per month: events created
(PostHog `event_created`).

## Not doing (and why)

- **Undisclosed "independent" accounts, fake testimonials, fake comments,
  bought followers:** against platform rules and advertising law (EU UCPD,
  Swiss UWG, FTC), and they poison the numbers we learn from.
- **Auto-posting tools that log in as the user, bots, auto-DMs:** account
  bans.
- **"Shot on LEFT" mark in the app:** postponed by the user (2026-11-18).
