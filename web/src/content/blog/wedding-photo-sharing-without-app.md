Wedding photo sharing without an app comes down to four options: a link to a shared cloud folder, a QR code that opens an upload page, a shared album on everyone's phones, or real disposable cameras. All of them work with nothing installed. The catch is the same in each case: the photos are taken at the wedding, but uploading is a separate chore guests have to remember afterwards. Pick the option that makes that second step smallest for your crowd.

## Why couples look for no-app photo sharing at a wedding

The worry is reasonable. A wedding has guests of every age and every phone, and an evening of dinner and dancing is not the time to ask anyone to create an account. Every extra step loses some people: the guest who has no storage left, the aunt who does not know her app store password, the friend whose phone is at 9 percent by 10 p.m.

So the goal is not "no app" for its own sake. It is the fewest steps between a guest taking a photo and that photo reaching you.

## Option 1: a link to a shared cloud folder

Create a folder in whatever cloud storage you already use, set it so anyone with the link can add files, and send the link out.

**Where it works:** small weddings, and guests who are comfortable with files. It costs nothing and you keep full-size originals.

**The catch:**

- Uploading from a phone into a folder takes several taps, and some services push guests towards their own app or a sign-in anyway.
- Nothing reminds anyone. Once the link sinks in the group chat, so do the photos.
- Anyone with the link can usually see, and sometimes delete, everything in it.

**Wording you can send the morning after:** "Thank you for yesterday. If you took any photos, we would love them in one place: [link]. Every blurry one counts."

## Option 2: a QR code to an upload page

A printed code on each table opens a web page where guests pick photos from their camera roll and send them. Many photo services and some wedding websites offer a page like this.

**Where it works:** guests who already have their phones out at dinner. Scanning a code is familiar to almost everyone now.

**The catch:**

- The code gets scanned during dinner, but the best photos are taken later, on the dance floor. The page is long closed by then.
- Choosing ten photos from a camera roll of three hundred is a chore guests put off.
- Put the code where people wait: the tables, the bar, the guest book. One sign at the entrance is easy to walk past.

For a printable layout and where to put the codes, see [QR code for wedding photos](/qr-code-for-wedding-photos/).

## Option 3: a shared album on everyone's phones

Phones come with shared albums built in. You create one, invite guests, and they add photos from their gallery.

**Where it works:** a family wedding where nearly everyone has the same kind of phone, or a small group of friends.

**The catch:**

- These albums work best inside one phone ecosystem. Guests on the other kind of phone often can only look, or need the other company's app, which is the thing you were avoiding.
- Shared albums can reduce the size of photos, which matters if you want prints.
- You need every guest's phone number or email to invite them, which means collecting contact details for a hundred people.

## Option 4: real disposable cameras

The only option with no phone at all. Put a camera on each table and collect them at the end of the night.

**Where it works:** couples who love the look of film, and crowds where many guests would never use their phone for this. Nothing to scan, nothing to upload, no phone battery to worry about.

**The catch:**

- Each camera has a fixed number of exposures, and someone has to collect and develop them all.
- Shots without the flash come out dark, and some cameras go home in a bag.
- You see the photos weeks later, after paying for developing.

For how many to buy, there is a table by guest count in [how many disposable cameras for a wedding](/blog/how-many-disposable-cameras-for-wedding/).

## How to choose

- **Under 30 guests, mostly friends:** a shared album or a cloud folder is enough.
- **Mixed ages, mixed phones:** a QR code plus a handful of disposable cameras for those who will not use their phone.
- **You want film and nothing digital:** disposable cameras, with one person in charge of collecting them.
- **You mostly want the dance floor:** anything that collects photos while they are taken, so nobody has to upload anything later.

Whatever you pick, three things raise the number of photos you get back. Tell guests once, out loud, during the toasts. Ask for something specific, such as "a photo of the people at your table", rather than "share your pics". And send the link again the morning after.
