To ask guests not to post wedding photos, say it in more than one place, say it kindly, and give them a date or a trigger to wait for: "Please hold off posting until we share our own photos on Sunday." Below is wording you can copy for the invitation, your website, a sign at the ceremony and the officiant's announcement, followed by what to do when someone posts anyway.

## Decide what you are actually asking

"No photos" and "no posting" are different requests, and guests need to know which one you mean. Pick one before you write anything:

- **Unplugged ceremony.** Phones away during the ceremony only. Guests can take photos at the party.
- **Wait to post.** Guests take photos all day but share nothing until you do.
- **No posting at all.** Photos are for guests to keep, and nothing goes on social media. This is the hardest to follow, so it needs the clearest wording.

Most couples want the second one. It protects the surprise of the first look, a private guest, or a family situation, and it asks very little of anyone.

## Wording for the invitation or wedding website

Put it where guests read the practical details, next to dress code and directions, not buried in the FAQ.

**Warm:**
"We would love for you to be fully with us. Please hold off posting photos of the day until we share ours on Sunday."

**Short:**
"Photos are welcome. Posting can wait until we have shared ours."

**Playful:**
"Take all the photos you like. Just keep them in your pocket until we say the film is developed."

**Direct, for a private reason:**
"For personal reasons we are asking guests not to post photos of the wedding on social media. Thank you for respecting that."

A reason helps, but you do not owe one. If you give it, keep it to a single sentence.

## Wording for a sign

A sign works at the ceremony entrance and near the guest book. Keep it to two lines that a person walking past can read:

- "Be here with us. Posting can wait."
- "Phones away for the ceremony. Photos welcome after."
- "We will share photos soon. Please wait for ours before you post."
- "Take photos, not to the feed. We will post first."

If you want something that says what to do instead of what not to do, finish with the useful part: where the photos go, and when they will see them.

## What the officiant can say

A guest who skipped the invitation will still hear this. Ask your officiant to say it once, before the ceremony starts, in a normal voice:

"Before we begin, the couple has asked for the ceremony to be unplugged. Please put your phones away and enjoy being here. There will be plenty of time for photos at the reception, and they would like you to wait before posting any of them online."

It takes twenty seconds and does more than three signs.

## Where to say it, in order of effect

1. The invitation or website, so it is known in advance.
2. The officiant, right before the ceremony.
3. A sign at the entrance.
4. A line from the person giving the welcome toast at the reception.

Saying it once in four places beats saying it loudly in one.

## Give guests something to do instead

A request with nothing behind it feels like a rule. Give guests a place for their photos and a time they will see them: "Send your photos to this link and we will all see the album on Sunday." People follow a request more easily when they get something for following it.

## If someone posts anyway

It happens at most weddings, and it is usually thoughtlessness, not defiance. Handle it like this:

1. **Message them privately, the same day.** Do not comment under the post.
2. **Keep it short:** "Hi, we are keeping photos offline until we share ours. Would you mind taking that one down? Thank you so much."
3. **Ask a friend to do it** if you do not want to be the one writing on your wedding weekend.
4. **Let it go if it is harmless.** A blurry shot of the cake that shows nobody is not worth a conflict.

You cannot control every phone in the room. What you can do is make the request clear and reasonable, so the guests who would follow it do.

## Where LEFT fits

LEFT is a camera that makes waiting part of the design. Guests join by link or QR code with no sign-up. Each gets a roll of a set length and every photo gets one film look. Nobody sees anyone else's photos until the film is developed at the time you choose, and then the whole group sees the album together.

That suits an "unplugged for now" request: guests still take photos, and there is nothing to post until the reveal. LEFT cannot stop anyone from using their phone's own camera, so the wording above is still worth putting on the invitation. More on how it works at a wedding is on the [wedding page](/wedding/).

If you want guests to keep shooting but wait to see anything, set up a film, put the QR code on the tables and say in one line when the photos will be developed.
